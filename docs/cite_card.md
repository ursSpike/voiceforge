## Dataset dependency (in the product)

### SpokenWOZ: A Large-Scale Speech-Text Benchmark for Spoken Task-Oriented Dialogue Agents
- **arXiv:** [2305.13040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13040) · Submitted 22 May 2023 (v1); last revised 24 Jun 2025 (v7)
- **Authors:** Shuzheng Si, Wentao Ma, Haoyu Gao, Yuchuan Wu, Ting-En Lin, Yinpei Dai, Hangyu Li, Rui Yan, Fei Huang, Yongbin Li
- **Verified finding:** Quoted from the abstract: "we introduce SpokenWOZ, a large-scale speech-text dataset for spoken TOD, containing 8 domains, 203k turns, 5.7k dialogues and 249 hours of audios from human-to-human spoken conversations." The abstract further reports verbatim that "the most advanced dialogue state tracker only achieves 25.65% in joint goal accuracy and the SOTA end-to-end model only correctly completes the user request in 52.1% of dialogues."
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** Direct dataset dependency. VoiceForge slices a small deterministic subset of SpokenWOZ's calls (word-level timestamps + speaker tags) as its public evaluation backbone. The paper is the SOURCE of the data; it does not evaluate, endorse, or validate VoiceForge.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** Cite-card line: "Our public backbone is SpokenWOZ (arXiv:2305.13040) — 8 domains, 203k turns, 5.7k dialogues, 249 hours; we slice a small deterministic subset." If asked whether the dataset is real: yes — published, CC BY-NC 4.0, with word-level timestamps, which is why timing is derived rather than fabricated. If asked how hard the problem is: quote the authors' own numbers (25.65% joint goal accuracy DST; 52.1% request completion SOTA end-to-end).
- **Limitation / non-claim:** SpokenWOZ is a DATASET DEPENDENCY, not a validation. VoiceForge does NOT reproduce this paper — it does not re-run SpokenWOZ baselines and does not claim to match or beat the 25.65% / 52.1% numbers (those are the authors' own). This paper does NOT justify VoiceForge's specific timing thresholds (e.g. 100ms/800ms); those are VoiceForge's own design choices, justified separately by deterministic-first design plus human calibration, not by appeal to this paper. Coverage caveats: SpokenWOZ is protocol-collected (latency-rich, not interruption-rich) and English-only; barge-in / multilingual coverage comes from other pool members.


## Motivation (why the problem matters — does NOT validate VoiceForge)

### τ-Voice: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Voice Agents on Real-World Domains
- **arXiv:** [2603.13686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13686) · 2026-03-14 (v1)
- **Authors:** Soham Ray, Keshav Dhandhania, Victor Barres, Karthik Narasimhan
- **Verified finding:** Quoted from the abstract: "We evaluate task completion (pass@1) and voice interaction quality across 278 tasks: while GPT-5 (reasoning) achieves 85%, voice agents reach only 31--51% under clean conditions and 26--38% under realistic conditions with noise and diverse accents--retaining only 30--45% of text capability; qualitative analysis confirms 79--90% of failures stem from agent behavior." Headline: on the same grounded tasks a strong text agent hits 85% pass@1 while voice agents hit only 31-51% clean / 26-38% realistic, retaining just 30-45% of text capability.
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** Independent peer benchmark that MOTIVATES why voice-agent evaluation matters by quantifying a large voice-vs-text capability gap. It does NOT validate VoiceForge: VoiceForge does not run τ²-bench, the τ-Voice simulator, GPT-5, or any of the 278 tasks, and shares only the problem framing, not the methodology or data.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** Pitch one-liner: "An independent 2026 benchmark, τ-Voice (arXiv:2603.13686), finds voice agents retain only 30-45% of text-mode capability across 278 grounded tasks — evaluating voice agents is a real, unsolved problem, which is the gap VoiceForge targets." If challenged that voice evals being hard is just opinion, cite their 85% (text) vs 31-51% / 26-38% (voice) split and the 79-90% agent-behavior failure share. Keep all numbers verbatim, no rounding.
- **Limitation / non-claim:** This paper does not validate VoiceForge and VoiceForge does not reproduce it (no τ²-bench run, no τ-Voice simulator, no GPT-5, none of the 278 tasks). None of its numbers measure VoiceForge's accuracy or correctness — it establishes that the problem matters, not that our solution works. It does NOT justify VoiceForge's specific deterministic thresholds (e.g. 100ms/800ms FTO bands); those are VoiceForge's own design choices. Scope differs: τ-Voice evaluates end-to-end agent task completion on a synthetic-simulator benchmark, whereas VoiceForge analyzes real call transcripts/timing for deterministic failure signals.

### VoiceAgentBench: Are Voice Assistants ready for agentic tasks?
- **arXiv:** [2510.07978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07978) · Submitted 9 Oct 2025 ([v1]); last revised 13 Feb 2026 ([v3])
- **Authors:** Dhruv Jain, Harshit Shukla, Gautam Rajeev, Ashish Kulkarni, Chandra Khatri, Shubham Agarwal
- **Verified finding:** Quoted from the abstract: "Across agentic tasks, ASR-LLM pipelines outperform end-to-end SpeechLMs, achieving up to 60.6% average parameter-filling accuracy on English, while SpeechLMs exhibit lower performance and sharper degradation on Indic languages. All models struggle in sequential workflows and safety evaluations, highlighting persistent limitations in tool orchestration, multilingual generalization, and safety robustness." The benchmark "compris[es] 6,000+ synthetic spoken queries ... across English and six Indic languages."
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** Motivation only. This paper frames the multilingual/Indic agentic-voice evaluation problem (existing speech benchmarks "do not systematically evaluate agentic behavior or adversarial robustness"). It does NOT validate VoiceForge; VoiceForge does not reproduce or score against this benchmark.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** In a demo, cite to answer "why does multilingual/Indic agentic-voice evaluation matter?" — a 6,000+ query benchmark across English and six Indic languages where the best pipeline reaches only up to 60.6% parameter-filling accuracy on English and shows sharper degradation on Indic languages. Use to motivate the problem space, not to claim VoiceForge solves or measures it.
- **Limitation / non-claim:** VoiceForge does NOT reproduce, re-run, or score against VoiceAgentBench, and makes no claim to its results (the 60.6% figure is the paper's own evaluated pipelines). This paper does NOT justify VoiceForge's specific thresholds (e.g., 100ms/800ms latency gates) — those are VoiceForge's own design choices. Honesty context: SpokenWOZ is a dataset dependency; the benchmark papers (this one) motivate but do not validate; judge-bias papers justify deterministic-first + human calibration.

### WildSpeech-Bench: Benchmarking End-to-End SpeechLLMs in the Wild
- **arXiv:** [2506.21875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21875) · Submitted 27 Jun 2025 (v1); last revised 26 Sep 2025 (v3)
- **Authors:** Linhao Zhang, Jian Zhang, Bokai Lei, Chuhan Wu, Aiwei Liu, Wei Jia, Xiao Zhou
- **Verified finding:** Quoted from the abstract: "the lack of specialized and comprehensive benchmarks for end-to-end speech LLM evaluation hinders optimizing the user experience of Audio LLMs in real-world applications. Existing evaluation methods often adapt text-based benchmarks, overlooking speech's unique characteristics and challenges, including prosody, homophones, stuttering, and differing user expectations." The abstract also states the authors "conduct comprehensive testing and detailed analysis of various mainstream speech models, revealing significant differences in model performance across different speech scenarios." Note: the abstract is qualitative and reports no single headline numeric statistic.
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** Motivation only — frames why text-adapted benchmarks miss speech-specific phenomena, motivating the need for real-world speech-LLM evaluation. Does NOT validate VoiceForge.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** Cite when asked why evaluating a speech LLM needs its own harness instead of reusing text benchmarks: the paper documents that existing methods adapt text-based benchmarks and thereby overlook speech-unique challenges (prosody, homophones, stuttering, user expectations). Use to motivate the problem space, not to claim VoiceForge solves or reproduces it.
- **Limitation / non-claim:** This paper MOTIVATES the problem; it does NOT validate VoiceForge. VoiceForge does not reproduce this benchmark, its dataset, or its results, and does not run WildSpeech-Bench or replicate its query-aware evaluation. It does NOT justify any of VoiceForge's specific thresholds (e.g. 100ms/800ms latency cutoffs). The abstract gives no numeric headline statistic, so no percentage/score should be attached to this citation.


## Judge-bias evidence (justifies deterministic-first + human calibration)

### A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge
- **arXiv:** [arXiv:2411.15594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594) · v1 — 23 Nov 2024 (latest v6 — 19 Oct 2025)
- **Authors:** Jiawei Gu, Xuhui Jiang, Zhichao Shi, Hexiang Tan, Xuehao Zhai, Chengjin Xu, Wei Li, Yinghan Shen, Shengjie Ma, Honghao Liu, Saizhuo Wang, Kun Zhang, Yuanzhuo Wang, Wen Gao, Lionel Ni, Jian Guo
- **Verified finding:** "However, ensuring the reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge systems remains a significant challenge that requires careful design and standardization." The abstract further frames its mitigation scope: "We explore strategies to enhance reliability, including improving consistency, mitigating biases, and adapting to diverse assessment scenarios." (Both exact quotes from the arXiv abstract page; reliability is treated as an open problem, with bias and consistency named explicitly as things to mitigate/improve.)
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** JUSTIFIES VoiceForge's deterministic-first stance plus human calibration. The survey treats LLM-as-judge reliability — specifically bias mitigation and consistency — as an open challenge requiring careful design and standardization, which supports leading with deterministic signals rather than trusting an LLM judge by default.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** If asked "why not just use an LLM to grade the outputs?", cite this survey: LLM-as-judge reliability is a documented open challenge (the literature itself calls for mitigating bias and improving consistency), which is exactly why VoiceForge grades deterministically first and uses human calibration on top.
- **Limitation / non-claim:** Justifies the deterministic-first + human-calibration posture only; does NOT validate, evaluate, or measure VoiceForge. Provides NO support for VoiceForge's exact thresholds (e.g. 100ms/800ms). VoiceForge does NOT reproduce this paper or its benchmark. The finding is qualitative framing, not a measured statistic. Not the SpokenWOZ dataset dependency and not one of the three benchmark problem-motivation papers.

### Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge
- **arXiv:** [2410.02736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736) · v1: 3 Oct 2024; v2: 4 Oct 2024
- **Authors:** Jiayi Ye, Yanbo Wang, Yue Huang, Dongping Chen, Qihui Zhang, Nuno Moniz, Tian Gao, Werner Geyer, Chao Huang, Pin-Yu Chen, Nitesh V. Chawla, Xiangliang Zhang
- **Verified finding:** Quoted from the abstract: "we identify 12 key potential biases and propose a new automated bias quantification framework-CALM-which systematically quantifies and analyzes each type of bias in LLM-as-a-Judge by using automated and principle-guided modification." The abstract further states that "while advanced models have achieved commendable overall performance, significant biases persist in certain specific tasks" and that "there remains room for improvement in the reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge." No single headline bias percentage is given in the abstract, so none is quoted.
- **Relationship to VoiceForge:** JUSTIFIES VoiceForge's deterministic-first design and blind-human-label calibration pilot. Because LLM judges carry 12 documented, persistent bias types, VoiceForge keeps load-bearing signals (barge-in, latency, turn accounting) deterministic and calibrates the LLM judge against blind human labels rather than trusting it. Does NOT validate VoiceForge or reproduce any result.
- **Demo / Q&A use:** Cite-card one-liner: "LLM-as-judge has 12 documented bias types (arXiv:2410.02736), and significant biases persist even in advanced models — which is why VoiceForge computes the deterministic signals itself and calibrates the LLM judge against blind human labels instead of trusting it outright." Answers the "why not just let the LLM grade everything?" question.
- **Limitation / non-claim:** Paper JUSTIFIES the deterministic-first + human-calibration posture; it does NOT validate VoiceForge, reproduce any VoiceForge result, or measure VoiceForge's own judge. It does NOT justify any specific VoiceForge threshold (e.g. 100ms/800ms) — those are VoiceForge's own design choices. We do not claim to reproduce CALM or its numbers.
