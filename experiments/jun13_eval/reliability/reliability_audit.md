# Judge-Reliability Audit (FROZEN labels × judge binary)

Agent 1 — Reliability Metrics. ISOLATED experiment, deterministic, stdlib-only.
Inputs (read-only): `eval/labels_spike.csv`, `out/judge_results.json`, `out/calls.json`.
Bootstrap: seed **13**, **2000** resamples, percentile method. Generated by
`reliability_audit.py`; machine-readable mirror in `reliability_audit.json`.

**Pairing rule.** A call enters the audit iff the human `primary_label ∈ {success, fail}`
**and** the judge binary label `∈ {success, fail}`. This yields **n = 45**. The single
human-`unsure` call (`swz_MUL0815`, judged `success`) is excluded by construction — exactly
as the task specifies. `fail` is treated as the **RISK / positive class** for
recall / precision / specificity / MCC.

## 1. What these numbers mean — read this first

`kappa`, `balanced_accuracy`, and the `confusion matrix` answer **three different questions.
They are reported side by side. The uncomfortable κ is NOT swapped for a flattering metric.**

| metric | question it answers | what it is blind to |
|---|---|---|
| **confusion matrix** | raw counts: how many of each (human, judge) cell? | nothing — it is the ground truth the others summarize |
| **raw agreement** | what fraction did they agree on? | chance agreement; inflated by class imbalance |
| **Cohen's κ** | agreement *beyond chance*, given each side's marginal rates | absolute performance; collapses under skewed prevalence |
| **balanced accuracy** | mean of failure-recall and specificity (per-class) | calibration vs chance; treats both classes as equally important |

At ~82% human-success prevalence, κ is **structurally compressed** (the prevalence paradox):
the same correct/incorrect calls can yield high raw agreement and a low κ. That is a property
of the metric, not new evidence about the judge. We report both and explain the gap rather
than picking whichever reads better.

## 5. FLAGGED: the dashboard's code-switch reliability claim is UNSUPPORTED

> **Current report/dashboard sentence:** *"9 of 13 disagreements are code-switched, so the
> judge is least reliable there."*

**The COUNT is true. The CONCLUSION does not follow.**

- Disagreements total: **13**. Of those, **9 are hi-en** and **4 are en**. So "9 of 13" is
  arithmetically correct.
- But reliability is a **RATE**, not a count:
  - **hi-en agreement: 22/31 = 71.0%**
  - **English (en) agreement: 9/13 = 69.2%**
- These rates are **~equal** (within ~2 points). The judge is **not** meaningfully *less*
  reliable on code-switched calls.
- The raw count of disagreements is a **base-rate artifact**: hi-en makes up **31 of 45** paired
  calls (69% of the set), so even at an *equal* error rate it will naturally accumulate the most
  disagreements. "Most errors happen where most calls are" is not evidence of a weak spot.

**Conclusion:** the "least reliable on code-switch" claim conflates a raw count with a rate and
should be removed or rewritten as "code-switched calls dominate the disagreement count purely
because they dominate the dataset; their agreement rate matches English."

### The better-supported slice (with its caveat)

The dimension that *does* separate reliability is **human confidence**:

- **high-confidence: 24/29 = 82.8%** agreement vs **medium-confidence: 8/16 = 50.0%.**
- That is a ~33-point gap — far larger and more credible than the ~2-point language gap.

**Caveat (do not over-claim):** annotator confidence is only known **after** annotation. It
is **not** available at inference time, so it **cannot drive an auto-router**. What it supports
is a **human REVIEW QUEUE** — surface the calls whose characteristics correlate with
low-confidence/medium-confidence labels for a second human pass — **not** an automated routing
decision. (Call-length, §4d, *is* knowable at inference time and shows a comparable spread;
it is the more actionable routing candidate, though the long bucket is specificity-only.)

## 6. Honesty notes

- **Counts vs rates are kept separate throughout.** Section 5 is the headline example; the
  same discipline applies to every slice table (fraction shown alongside rate).
- **Single-class slices are labeled as such.** Any slice with zero human-fails cannot define
  recall; zero human-successes cannot define specificity. We do not impute or hide these.
- **Small-n is never ranked.** Every slice with n<5 carries the explicit "not a finding" mark.
- **No claim is made that n cannot support.** Every overall metric with a meaningful CI shows
  one; the κ / balanced-accuracy / MCC CIs all include or nearly include their null, which we
  state plainly rather than rounding away.
